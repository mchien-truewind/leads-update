# Truewind BDR Qualification Playbook

Use this on every first-touch discovery call. The goal is not to run a product tour. The goal is to decide whether the lead is worth AE time, identify the exact workflow pain, and leave the call with a committed next step or a clean no.

This guide is based on 25 anonymized first-touch Grain discovery calls, HubSpot outcome checks where available, Sandler qualification principles, and current BDR discovery best practices. Do not paste raw transcript notes, prospect names, or company names into handoffs.

## Great Lead Definition

A great Truewind lead has all four:

- Supported accounting stack: Sage Intacct, QuickBooks Online, NetSuite, or a confirmed migration to one of them.
- Real accounting complexity: multi-entity close, high transaction volume, document-heavy reconciliations, AP matching, prepaid schedules, roll-forwards, flux analysis, or audit support.
- Lean finance capacity: in-house accounting team of 3 or more, or an accounting/CAS firm managing 10 or more clients on supported GLs.
- Reachable authority: CFO, Controller, Head of Finance, CAS leader, firm owner, or managing partner is on the call or committed to the next call.

Priority signals:

- Upcoming audit, board reporting deadline, ERP migration, headcount freeze, headcount replacement, or recent finance leadership change.
- Incumbent process/tool failure.
- Buyer names a painful workflow before being prompted.
- Buyer asks integration, security, or implementation questions.
- Warm referral, conference/webinar inbound, or existing-customer referral.

## Stop Rules

Do not book an AE demo if any hard stop is present:

- No supported GL or the GL decision is still unresolved.
- No economic buyer reachable within one call cycle.
- No budget path and no forcing function.
- Finance function is too small or fully outsourced with no internal owner.
- The conversation is about partnership, M&A, recruiting, or research rather than buying.
- Compliance or integration blocker is surfaced and cannot be cleared before a demo.

Soft disqualify if two or more are true:

- Buyer says they are "just exploring."
- Timeline is "sometime later" with no business trigger.
- Pain is vague and cannot be tied to hours, risk, delayed reporting, or headcount.
- Prospect is building internally and cannot name what would make them switch.
- Decision-maker is absent and the champion cannot explain how to bring them in.

## Exact Call Script

### 1. Upfront Contract

Say this verbatim:

> Thanks for making time today. Here is how I suggest we use the next 30 minutes. I will ask a handful of questions about your close process, accounting stack, and where the friction is. You can ask me anything about Truewind. At the end, we should be able to decide one of three things: there is enough fit to schedule a working demo, it is not the right fit, or there is something specific we need to confirm before going further. Does that work for you?

Why: this follows Sandler's upfront-contract habit. It prevents vague "send me info" endings and gives both sides permission to qualify out.

### 2. Context

Ask:

> Walk me through your month-end close today. Who owns it, what systems are involved, and roughly how long does it take?

Listen for: ERP/GL, team size, outsourced vs in-house, close cadence, reporting deadline, and whether the buyer can explain the workflow clearly.

### 3. Pain

Ask:

> Where does that process actually break down?

Then ask:

> What happens when that breaks? Does it cost hours, delay reporting, create audit risk, or force the team into extra review work?

Do not accept "it is manual" as a complete answer. Manual work only matters if the buyer can connect it to time, risk, cost, reporting delay, or headcount pressure.

### 4. Stack And Fit

Ask:

> What GL or ERP are you running today, and is that system live or still being evaluated?

Then ask:

> What other tools touch the workflow: AP automation, expense, billing, payroll, reconciliations, or close management?

If they are mid-ERP evaluation, unsupported, or dependent on a tool conflict, flag it before booking a demo.

### 5. Budget And Authority

Ask directly:

> For a tool like this, who owns the decision and budget?

Then:

> Is budget already set aside for this kind of workflow automation, or would you need to create the business case?

If budget is not confirmed:

> What would need to be true for budget to get approved, and who would need to sign off?

This is a BDR qualification gate. Do not push budget discovery to the AE.

### 6. Decision Process

Ask:

> If this looked like a fit, what would the buying process look like from here?

Then:

> Who could block this even if you wanted to move forward?

On multi-attendee calls, ask each person:

> What role would you play in evaluating or approving a tool like this?

Silent stakeholders are either champions, blockers, or budget holders. Do not leave them unidentified.

### 7. Timeline

Ask:

> Is there a deadline driving this, such as an audit, board review, close target, ERP migration, headcount decision, or client growth?

Strong answer: named deadline or business trigger.

Weak answer: "eventually," "just exploring," or "no real timeline."

### 8. Competition And Internal Build

Ask:

> What else are you considering: another tool, an internal build, an outsourced provider, or staying with the current process?

If they are building internally:

> What would make you stop building internally and choose an outside solution instead: speed, accuracy, cost, integrations, or something else?

### 9. Demo Gate

Only offer a demo if pain, stack, authority, budget path, and timeline are clear.

Say:

> Based on what you shared, I think the strongest fit is around [specific workflow pain]. The right next step is not a generic demo. It is a working session focused on [workflow], with [economic buyer or required stakeholder] in the room. Can we schedule 45 minutes for [date/time]?

If they will not commit:

> What would need to be true before that meeting makes sense?

If the answer is vague, do not mark the lead qualified.

## Qualification Scorecard

Score each category 0-2. A lead must score 8 or higher to route to AE.

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stack fit | Unsupported or unknown | Supported but not confirmed live | Supported and confirmed live |
| Pain | Vague | Named workflow | Named workflow plus measurable cost/risk |
| Complexity | Low volume/simple close | Some complexity | Multi-entity, high volume, audit, AP, recs, or firm-client scale |
| Authority | Unknown | Champion only | Economic buyer present or committed |
| Budget | No path | Path unclear | Budget or approval process mapped |
| Timeline | None | Soft target | Hard forcing function |

Routing:

- 10-12: Hot. Book AE demo within 5 business days.
- 8-9: Warm. BDR must close missing budget/buyer/timeline gap before demo.
- 5-7: Nurture. No AE handoff.
- 0-4: Disqualify.

## BDR Coaching Rules

- Do not demo before qualification. Across reviewed calls, weak calls moved to product too early and missed budget, authority, or timeline.
- Time-box rapport to 3 minutes. Then use the upfront contract to pivot.
- Ask budget every time. Avoiding budget was the most consistent gap in the reviewed calls.
- Name the workflow pain in the next-step ask. "General Truewind overview" is not a qualified demo.
- Bring the economic buyer into the next call. If the buyer is not on the first call and cannot be named, the lead is not AE-ready.
- Treat compliance and integration issues as gates, not footnotes.
- End with a calendar commitment or a clear disqualification. "Send me times" is not a next step.

## Methodology Notes

- Sandler lens: upfront contract, pain, budget, decision process, fulfillment, and a clear yes/no next step.
- MEDDIC lens: metrics, economic buyer, decision criteria, decision process, identified pain, and champion.
- BDR lens: qualify before pitching, confirm understanding, ask layered follow-ups, and disqualify early when fit is weak.
